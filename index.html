How I work

A process that survives real teams.

No hero-designer fantasy. Five practical stages that keep research, design and delivery moving together.

Understand the problem before the pixels.

I start with the people who use the product and the people who run the business. Stakeholder interviews, analytics, support tickets and a competitive review give me a honest picture of what is actually broken.

You get

  • Stakeholder interviews
  • Analytics review
  • Competitive audit
  • Problem statement

Turn ambiguity into a shared map.

Personas, journeys and task flows make the problem concrete for the whole team. This is where scope gets honest: what we are solving, what we are not, and how we will know it worked.

You get

  • Personas
  • Journey maps
  • Information architecture
  • Success measures

Design the flow, then the screen.

Low-fidelity wireframes keep the conversation on structure. Once the flow holds, I move into high-fidelity UI, reusable components and a design system that can carry the next ten features.

You get

  • Wireframes
  • Interaction specs
  • High-fidelity UI
  • Component library

Test with people, not opinions.

Prototypes go in front of real users early enough to change the design. I run moderated and unmoderated tests, synthesise what I hear, and bring stakeholders into the room for design reviews.

You get

  • Clickable prototype
  • Usability tests
  • Findings summary
  • Design review

Stay close to the build.

Good design dies in handoff if nobody follows it. I write clear specs, pair with engineers, review the implementation and keep refining after release based on real usage.

You get

  • Dev-ready specs
  • Implementation QA
  • Release notes
  • Iteration plan
